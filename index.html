About

I'm a third-year Ph.D. candidate at Texas State University. I study hallucinations in generative AI — when models produce confident but wrong output — and build methods to detect and correct them in text, vision-language, and audio-language models.

My work covers evidence-based correction of LLM text, faithful image captions for safety-critical scenes, adversarial attacks that trigger hallucination, and clinical audio benchmarks. I expect to graduate in 2027.

Open to research internships and collaborations.

Research

Text

Breaking text into claims, checking them against evidence, and fixing only the wrong parts.

Vision-language

Short, faithful image captions so that images generated from them match the real scene.

Audio-language

Benchmarks for speech-based models, tracing how transcription errors silently affect answers in clinical settings.

Adversarial robustness

Prompt attacks that cause hallucination, and evaluations that hold up under attack and data shift.

Selected results

  • 76.8% correction accuracy on paragraph-level misinformation, with FactCC above 0.97 (1,400+ passages).
  • 92.5% peak success rate of weak-semantic prompt attacks in inducing hallucination.
  • 83% F1 on extractive QA (SQuAD, Natural Questions), 20+ points above TF-IDF baselines.
  • +30% BER improvement and 25% spectral-efficiency gain for 6G resource allocation under high mobility.
